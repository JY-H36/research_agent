## I. INTRODUCTION

Although advancements in speech synthesis and voice conversion have brought about convenience, they have also introduced the security risk of audio deepfake [1]-[3]. Partially deepfake audio, as a more covert and subtle type of audio deepfake, has recently attracted growing attention. In audio forensics, it is crucial to precisely identify and localize manipulated regions within partially deepfake audio, as these regions can provide vital information and evidence [4].

Existing partially deepfake audio localization models are primarily categorized into two tasks: manipulated region detection [5]-[7] and boundary detection [8]-[10]. Apart from improved localization, the domain shift commonly observed in partially deepfake audio across datasets also needs to be addressed [13]. Since manipulated segments and non-manipulated segments of partially deepfake audio generally come from different distributions, the model can easily learn to distinguish their differences using domain-specific features and perform well on datasets within the same domain. However, these domain-specific features can't represent the manipulated/non-manipulated segments of other distributions, causing the model to fail in real-world scenarios. To this end, previous works often leveraged standard data augmentation methods to enhance the generalization abilities of partially deepfake audio localization models, such as re-splicing audio, adding noise, and processing audio segments with new voice conversion models [11], [14], [15].

To address the domain shift and utilize the information from both manipulated regions and boundaries, we propose to use Adversarial training and Gradient Optimization (AGO) for partially deepfake audio localization. Adversarial training makes the model domain-invariant, and Gradient Optimization improves partially deepfake audio localization by reducing conflicts during the learning of both types of information. Specifically, we employ two branch networks: one is designed for domain classification, while the other handles two detection tasks-manipulated region detection and boundary detection. The domain classification branch uses a gradient reversal layer (GRL) to minimize its dependency on domain-specific features [18]. The branch of two detection tasks has two classifiers: one focuses on distinguishing between manipulated and non-manipulated regions, while the other specializes in detecting the boundaries of manipulated regions.

## III. EXPERIMENT SETUP

We evaluate our approach on two mainstream partially deepfake audio datasets: PartialSpoof [21] dataset and ADD2023 track 2 (ADD) dataset [20]. PartialSpoof has 25380 samples in the training set and 71237 samples in the test set. The ADD2023 challenge [20] introduced a partially deepfake audio Localization track, emphasizing the evaluation of partially deepfake audio localization models in cross-domain scenarios. The training set of ADD is derived from the HAD dataset [24], while the test set comprises 50,000 samples of genuine and partially deepfake audio from six diverse datasets, each of which includes added noise. For adversarial training, we randomly selected 1/10 of the unlabeled test samples from two datasets for access, reserving all samples for evaluation.

We employ mainstream evaluation metrics to assess model performance: Accuracy (Acc) and F 1 [22], and supplement with additional metrics such as EER, Precision, Recall, and Score [20] as used in various other papers.

---

additional metrics such as EER, Precision, Recall, and Score [20] as used in various other papers.

We trained the model using the Adam optimizer with a learning rate η of 0.0001 and a batch size of 16 on an NVIDIA A100 80GB GPU. The parameters of Wav2vec 2.0 were initialized from the pre-trained model XLSR-53, and the minimal frame of input audio was set to 40ms. The parameter α of the total loss function is set to 0.5.

## B. Ablation Study

As shown in Table II, we evaluated the contributions of adversarial training (AT) and gradient optimization (GO) to the model's performance. The results show that removing AT led to a drop in accuracy from 86.98% to 79.52% and a decline in F1 score from 71.87% to 57.03%, indicating that AT plays a crucial role in mitigating domain shifts and enhancing the model's generalization across different datasets. When GO was removed, the accuracy decreased to 81.34% and the F1 score dropped to 65.36%, demonstrating GO's effectiveness in alleviating gradient conflicts between the manipulated region detection and boundary detection tasks, thereby improving localization performance. The combined removal of AT and GO resulted in the most significant performance degradation, with accuracy falling to 77.03% and the F1 score to 52.66%. These findings highlight that the integration of AT and GO significantly enhances the model's robustness in cross-domain tasks and facilitates efficient information sharing between tasks.

## V. CONCLUSION

We introduced AGO, a novel method for partially deepfake audio localization, which integrates adversarial training with a gradient reversal layer to enhance the model's generalization under cross-domain conditions. By employing gradient optimization with orthogonal gradient descent, AGO effectively mitigates conflicts between manipulated region detection and boundary detection, allowing for seamless fusion of critical information from both tasks to enhance localization accuracy. The experiment results of AGO demonstrate a 22.82% improvement over the first-ranked method on the ADD2023 Track 2 dataset and establish a new state-ofthe-art on the PartialSpoof dataset, affirming the robustness and effectiveness of our approach.
